# Blog 7 - On AI and Math (10/16/2026)

> This will be quite different than my traditional blog, apologies for that.
> 
> Origially written 6/21/2026

We are of course on the onset of a new era of human intellectual endeavors: with AI growing increasingly powerful each day some have started to lose hope in pursuing higher intellectual activities. Of course, this is a very complicated topic. I'll motion into what I believe to be a likely scenario (in particular, I do not believe that AI will completely take over in any sense). To clarify, I define "intellectual endeavors" as some sort of higher educational pursuit. Examples include: getting a degree in biology, coding a website, engaging in a debate about ethics. 

It is hard to say how good AI will actually get. Some people are already saying that AI is slowing down, while others claim that we have not even scratched the surface. We that the AI will continue improving for a reasonable time period as it is better to cover a worse case scenario rather than just hoping for the best. 

Now, the most common concern is by far the issue of jobs. Many people believe that AI will completely replace people out in some jobs. This will be unfortunately true for some people. For example, AI is already replacing a lot of front-end developers' jobs. But, it is not as simple as AI instantly phasing them out. Notably, two responses arise which I believe would generalize in some way for every job in some danger:
The front-end developers adopted AI.
A stigma is created against AI, leading to demand of people who still have the required skills.
I believe that for most jobs in danger of AI, the above two will be the likely outcomes (and of course, a mix of both is possible and the most likely to occur). If I had to say, tasks that concern pure problem-solving will lean towards group 1 and more humanities based tasks will fall more towards group 2. 

Unfortunately, jobs will definitely be more scarce. Group 1 would be able to cover a lot more labor with far less people. Some jobs will change so radically that they will no longer be recognizable (assuming this scenario occurs). I will motivate this explanation with an example from history:

Believe it or not: "computer" used to be a job. A "computer" would sit down at a desk, receive some batch of instructions, and perform difficult computations by hand and return the results. This was how algorithms and complex calculations were done before electronic computers were made. We still observe a massive paradigm change with the onset of the (electronic) computer. Gradually, the human computer position would be completely wiped from existence. Now, the elimination of the human computer did not end the endeavors of complex calculations and algorithms. Rather, a whole new class of jobs were created that allowed for even more development.

But, there is one scary warning that the computer taught us about. That is, the new jobs required a completely new and foreign skill to the human computers. Instead of mindlessly following instructions, one had to be able to create and fix the instructions themselves. It is not inconceivable that AI might cause a similar paradigm change. Indeed, education and intellectual pursuits might be completely foreign to us in 20-30 years. 

This is to be taken with a grain of salt; the computer example came from a society that was quite different from what we have today. Additionally, the way AI is replacing humans is not the same as how the electronic computers replaced the human ones. So, there is a bit of difference here. But it is certainly something to consider (after all, this entire yap session is just a thought exercise). 

Perhaps another issue that isn't as talked about is that of laziness. Back to the group 1 vs. group 2 analogy: we are sometimes losing something with the group 1 people. Basic knowledge concerning a subject is sometimes frighteningly lacking. Of course, AI enthusiasts claim that no such knowledge is needed. But many intellectual endeavors are fundamentally about understanding: losing that would damage the point of such a field (e.g., most of academia). Even so, it is important even if you are a developer or a quant to understand what your AI buddy is actually doing. 

Now, the dependence on AI isn't all that big of a deal. To think such would be called a "faux neo" issue (as VSauce coined): people have been dependent on other sources without critically thinking for themselves since the dawn of time.

Regardless, it is important to think of what paradigm changes AI will bring. Whether AI becomes capable enough to bring it is an entirely different question. One should be mindful about how they are using AI and also consider how it may impact their future career.
